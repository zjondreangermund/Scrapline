# Roadmap

## Working rules
Complete one milestone, verify it, and record the result before moving to the next. Keep mechanical and visual customization throughout development. Do not claim production graphics, phone performance, or completed features without build evidence.

## Ordered delivery
1. Visual and performance proof: one polished car, garage, touch camera, paint/wheels, removable bonnet, device profiling.
2. Workshop: part catalogue, unique inventory instances, condition, compatibility, fitting, disassembly, repair, local save.
3. Salvage: compact exploration area, donor car, recovery, dismantling recipes.
4. Drag racing: vehicle simulation, launch and shifts, traction, telemetry, timing, AI opponent.
5. AI mechanic: structured diagnosis, inventory-aware repair options, offline fallback; optional backend language layer later.
6. First playable polish: tutorials, audio, accessibility, quality tiers, device stability, complete loop verification.
7. Expansion after review: more cars, engine swaps, deeper components, customization catalogue, rival progression, championships.
8. Later investigation: online races and authoritative validation.

## Decisions to resolve through prototypes
- Engine and rendering pipeline.
- Art direction and licensed asset sources.
- Granularity of mechanical simulation and repair animations.
- Controls and camera behavior on small touch screens.
- Supported device tiers and sustainable rendering budgets.

## Immediate next task
Produce the garage/car visual proof and measure it on the target phone before building the larger world. Keep the implementation scope small enough to replace inadequate art or rendering choices early.
